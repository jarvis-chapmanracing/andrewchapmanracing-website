# Lead Rubric

Use this rubric to judge whether a sponsorship lead is worth pursuing for Andrew Chapman Racing.

## Core company profile
Prefer companies that are:
- startups
- mid-size brands
- actively trying to grow
- likely to have a real marketing budget
- capable of supporting at least one realistic sponsorship structure:
  - single-race
  - in-kind
  - season-long

## Strong fit signals
Prioritize companies that benefit from:
- brand awareness
- social media/content exposure
- brand fit with racing
- community presence

Race-market proximity is preferred but not required.

Do not over-bias toward only racing or automotive companies. Existing motorsports fit is useful, but not required.

## Hard disqualifiers
Reject companies that are:
- too small
- showing no visible marketing activity or spend
- inactive
- impractical to structure a deal with
- sketchy or low quality

Large national brands are not automatic rejects. Evaluate them case by case, but do not prioritize them unless they are clearly reachable and realistic.

## Contact targeting rules
Choose contact targets based on company size.

### Smaller companies
Prefer:
- founder
- owner

### Bigger startups and mid-size companies
Prefer:
- CMO
- marketing director

### Secondary targets
Use if the preferred role is unavailable:
- CEO
- president
- other marketing employees
- other clear decision-makers

### Weak fallback targets
Use only when stronger paths are unavailable:
- sales manager
- relevant manager

### Do not count these as real targets
- info@
- generic sales inboxes
- HR inboxes
- low-level employees without decision power

## Contact path ranking
Use the highest-confidence route to the right person.

1. direct company email to the named contact
2. LinkedIn profile or message path
3. Instagram DM
4. phone number

Interpret these strictly:
- direct email to the named contact is the gold standard
- LinkedIn and phone can justify more research, but usually not a strong lead on their own
- Instagram is weaker still and should not rescue a weak lead
- contact form is ineffective by default
- generic company email is ineffective by default
- portal-only access is weak by default and often awful

## Contact verification workflow
1. Verify with public sources first.
   - official company site
   - official press release
   - current LinkedIn profile
   - official social account
2. Use Apollo or other enrichment tools second.
   - confirm current employment
   - confirm title accuracy
   - validate direct email
   - cross-check whether phone or LinkedIn really ties to that person
3. Assign the final V-score after combining both layers.
4. If sources conflict, prefer stronger current evidence and downgrade confidence.

## Contact verification scale
Judge the path itself, not just the company.

### V0 - Unverified
- guessed email pattern
- generic inbox
- portal only
- stale or uncertain LinkedIn
- generic switchboard
- unclear Instagram ownership

### V1 - Weakly verified
- person likely exists at the company
- title loosely matches
- path is inferred or weakly tied

### V2 - Moderately verified
- person clearly works there now
- title is relevant
- path is likely real from at least one credible source

### V3 - Strongly verified
- direct evidence the path goes to that person now
- official page, official PR, current profile, direct line, or other strong public proof

### V4 - Confirmed
- strongest current proof
- often multiple reliable sources or an official listing that clearly names the person and path

## Verification by channel
### Email
- V0: generic inbox or guessed pattern only
- V2: direct email found in one credible source or one strong enrichment source
- V3-V4: direct email found on official site, official PR, or confirmed across multiple reliable sources including Apollo or another enrichment tool

### LinkedIn
- V0: stale or uncertain profile
- V2: current role appears valid
- V3-V4: current active profile with clear role relevance and recency, ideally cross-checked by enrichment data

### Phone
- V0: switchboard only
- V2: likely department or business number
- V3-V4: direct line, extension, or founder-owner business line for a small company, ideally supported by public or enrichment evidence

### Instagram
- V0: unclear or inactive account
- V2: official active brand or founder account
- V3-V4: official active account clearly useful for business contact, ideally supported by matching public identity data

## Act-now signals
Prioritize leads when they have:
- proximity to an upcoming race
- a stronger or easier contact path
- a contact more likely to respond
- a higher likelihood of creating a deal
- stronger brand fit

## Required lead fields
Always keep:
- company name
- company size
- industry category
- realistic deal type
- stage
- primary contact name
- primary contact title
- primary contact path
- contact path type
- contact verification level
- verification evidence
- enrichment evidence used
- best outreach method
- notes
- source links

Optional:
- website
- location
- why it fits
- why now
- backup contact
- backup contact path
- backup verification level
- backup verification evidence

## Stage gates

### Candidate
Use when the company appears promising, but research is still incomplete.

### Vetted
Use when company fit is confirmed and worth pursuing, but the right person or route is still incomplete.

### Pitch-ready
Use only when:
- the right person is identified
- the contact path points directly to that person
- the best path is verified at V3 or better
- the opportunity is ready for outreach

In practice, this usually means a direct email to the named contact.

## Mistakes to avoid
- adding junk to hit volume
- trusting generic inboxes
- accepting named contacts without direct paths to those contacts
- treating portal-only access as strong
- treating missing contact names as acceptable
- confusing company quality with contact verification quality
- chasing companies that are too small and unrealistic
- prioritizing flashy brands that are not actually reachable
- forgetting that the goal is a real company Andrew can genuinely pitch a deal to
