# Lead Qualification

Use this skill to evaluate sponsorship leads for Andrew Chapman Racing.

Keep the workflow strict:
1. Screen the company first.
2. Keep only companies that are worth pursuing.
3. Find the right person only after the company passes screening.
4. Treat a lead as pitch-ready only when the right person and a usable contact path are identified.

## Workflow

### 1. Screen the company
Prioritize:
- startups
- mid-size brands
- companies that are actively trying to grow
- companies that could realistically support one of these deal types:
  - single-race
  - in-kind
  - season-long

Preferred fit signals:
- near an upcoming ARCA West race market
- visible marketing activity, sponsorship activity, events, or community promotion
- strong brand fit with Andrew Chapman Racing
- clear value from brand awareness, social media/content, or community presence
- credible public image on the car and across social channels

Do not require:
- Arizona or California location by itself
- existing motorsports or automotive affiliation by itself

### 2. Exclude weak companies early
Reject companies that are:
- too small
- showing no visible marketing spend
- inactive
- impractical for any realistic sponsorship structure
- sketchy or low quality

Treat giant national brands case by case. Keep them only when there is a credible reason they are reachable or relevant.

### 3. Choose the right contact by company size
For smaller companies, prefer:
- founder
- owner

For bigger startups and mid-size companies, prefer:
- CMO
- marketing director

Fallbacks, in order:
- CEO or president
- other marketing employees
- other clear decision-makers
- sales manager or other relevant managers only if stronger options are unavailable

Avoid treating these as useful contacts:
- info@
- generic sales inboxes
- generic HR inboxes
- low-level employees without decision power

### 4. Rank contact paths
Use this order of trust:
1. Direct company email
2. LinkedIn profile or message path
3. Phone number
4. Instagram DM

Treat these as not meaningful for pitch-ready status:
- contact forms
- generic company emails

### 5. Apply stage definitions
Use these gates exactly.

#### Candidate
A company looks like a possible fit, but it has not been fully researched.

#### Vetted
The company fit is confirmed and worth pursuing, but the right person or route is still incomplete.

#### Pitch-ready
The right person is identified, the contact is identified, and the opportunity is ready for outreach.

## Output expectations
When qualifying a lead, clearly provide:
- company name
- company type and size impression
- why it fits or fails
- deal type(s) that seem realistic
- current stage: Candidate, Vetted, or Pitch-ready
- target contact and why that person is right
- best contact path and backup path
- key risks or gaps
